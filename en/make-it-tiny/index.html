Why a task can feel impossible to start

When a task feels big, the next step your mind pictures is the whole thing: the finished video, the clean flat, the sent application. Starting then feels like signing up for all of it at once, and it is easy to freeze, especially if you are sensitive to pressure or tend to overthink.

Make It Tiny separates the two. You name the big thing, set it aside for now, and choose one step small enough to finish in about ten minutes. Starting becomes cheap, and once you have started, the next step is usually easier to see.

How to use it

  1. Write down the thing you want to do but can't start.
  2. Choose the smallest step you could finish in ten minutes, small enough that failing would feel almost silly.
  3. Do only that step, then mark it done. Stop there, or shrink the next one.
